</p>
<p>Tall, strong and impatient, it is told that Moses the Black was so unruly that his Egyptian Slavemaster freed him into the desert to be rid of him, but Moses himself claimed to have escaped alone. Nevertheless, Moses joined a band of robbers to survive and quickly proved his fervour, quickly becoming the leader of dozens.</p>
<p>The band of thieves pillaged their way around Egypt, with the tall Moses of Ethipian origin becoming a fearful figure to the Egyptian villagers. At one point, Moses swam across the Nile to chase a villager. And when the villager escaped, Moses slaughtered his sheep and dragged two of the carcasses back across the river again for supper.</p>
<p>But despite the terror Moses inflicted around the land, he yearned for something greater. He prayed to idols as to whether they were gods, but they were silent. He prayed to the sun as to whether it was a god, but it was silent. Then one day in the Egyptian desert he came across a monastery, and upon entering inside he came across the monks and their abbot Isidore. Despite Moses' stature and infamy, the monks who had no concern for their material possessions or welfare welcomed him and showed compassion.</p>
<p>Seeing the piety of the monks, Moses refused to leave, demanding to be made a monk. Upon confessing all of the sins that would come from many years of leading a roving band of thieves, the monks witnessed an angel striking the man's words from off a stone tablet one by one.</p>
<p>Nonetheless, seeing an impatient soul, Isadore sat down with Moses for an immensely exhausting prayer vigil, forcing the robber to resist distraction until the sun rose the next day. “Only over time does one grow to become a true contemplative,” said the abbot to Moses. And after four years of worship, teaching and many more tests, the man who had been so feared over all of Egypt tempered his desires and earnt his place in the monastery.</p>
<p>But his fervor and strength were not gone from him, only redirected to God, and despite choosing a cell far from water, Moses used the strength that he had previously used to chase down victims and carry heavy spoils to instead fetch water over long distances to provide for the elderly monks who could not carry water themselves. </p>
<p>One day, a small group of robbers made the mistake of breaking into Moses' cell. The other monks were pulled from their prayer by the surprising sight of Monk Moses dragging four rope-bound thieves into the main monastery. Now that he was a monk, Moses could not kill the thieves, but did not know what to do with them. Moses was told to call the thieves to repentance. The thieves then recognised the enormous monk as their very own missing leader, and in time were convinced to become monks themselves.</p>
<p>As Moses grew in wisdom, he was given more reponsibilities, including council decisions. One day, when called to decide the consequences for a monk who had broken monastic rules, Moses was nowhere to be seen. The council sent younger monks three times to fetch Moses, but all came back alone. Finally, Moses was heard, but instead of walking in and taking his seat, he came in slowly, carrying on his back a container full of sand that leaked along the ground behind him.  </p>
<p>"My sins run out behind me, and I do not see them, and I come this day to judge my brother..."</p>
<p>In awe of Moses' humility, the council adjourned, and no punishment was handed out that day.</p>
<p>Many more miracles and teachings of Moses occured throughout his life, and he was  ordained a priest and promoted to the abbot in his later years, but the culmination of his journey from sinner to saint came on the day of his death. Once again threatened by thieves, but this time too many for an old Moses to wrestle into ropes, the monastery had to be abandoned. Moses himself, however, was to stay behind.</p>
<p>"All who take up the sword will perish by the sword," Moses quoted from the book of Matthew.</p>
<p>Several other monks also chose to stay with Abbot Moses, but one eventually hid himself, frightened by the approaching thieves, and was thus able to tell of Moses' final end.</p>
<p>These thieves would not repent, and slew all of the monks and Abbot Moses that day, and the hidden monk witnessed the crown of martyrdom alight from heaven and rest upon the head of St Moses the Black.</p>
